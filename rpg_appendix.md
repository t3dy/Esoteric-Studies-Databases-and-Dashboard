# Appendix: Research Cognition vs. Radiant Storytelling

You are building a **Research Cognition Engine**, but your design desires share a striking "DNA" with modern high-level RPG (Role-Playing Game) system design, specifically **Radiant Storytelling** (as seen in games like *Skyrim* or *Elden Ring*).

## 1. The World-State as Data (Relational Logic)
- **Research Desire**: Linking 1,600 PDFs to 249 chats to 50 scholars.
- **RPG Designer Desire**: Linking 1,000 NPCs to 500 locations to 1,000 quests.
- **The Parallel**: Both require a **Relational Schema**. If you kill an NPC (or delete a Scholar), the "World State" must reflect that change across all dependencies. You aren't just storing nodes; you're storing **Relationships**.

## 2. Emergent Narrative (Inquiry Density)
- **Research Desire**: Visualizing "Popularity Contests" and "Inquiry Density."
- **RPG Designer Desire**: Tracking "Player Interest" and "Faction Aggro."
- **The Parallel**: Radiant storytelling systems don't script every event; they **monitor metrics** (player behavior) and trigger "encounters." Your "Popularity Contest" is effectively the "Action Index" of your research world. It tells the system: "The player is spending a lot of time in the *Alchemy* biome; spawn more scholars."

## 3. The "Journal" as a Knowledge Base
- **Research Desire**: The Chat Archive with exegesis and citations.
- **RPG Designer Desire**: The Lore Codex / Quest Log.
- **The Parallel**: A good RPG system doesn't just say "Goal: Kill Dragon." It says "This dragon was seen in the *Libri Secretorum*." By archiving your chats with **Provenance** (Source links), you are building an **Evergreen Lore Engine** that allows you to "re-read" the history of your own thought.

## 4. Branching Outcomes (Version 2 Strategy)
- **Research Desire**: Branching features so one can be reverted without killing the project.
- **RPG Designer Desire**: Branching questlines where one choice doesn't lock out the entire game.
- **The Parallel**: This is **Modular Architecture**. By moving to a more formal, branch-based engineering strategy, you are essentially creating "Save Points" in your architectural evolution.

## 5. From Database to Engine
You said it perfectly: "You are not building a CRUD app."
- **The CRUD App**: A static shop window. (Inventory management).
- **The Engine**: A simulation. (Radiant Storytelling).

Your library is becoming a **Simulation of Scholar Presence**. Every time you add a scholar ID and a question move, you are "spawning" a data connection that can trigger new research "quests."
